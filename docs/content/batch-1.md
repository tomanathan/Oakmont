### Traps added to the lesson

- Running two independent clauses together with no punctuation at all (a fused sentence).
- Pairing a semicolon with 'and,' 'but,' or 'so' ('; and'), or moving the comma after the conjunction ('and,') — the pattern is a comma before the conjunction, or a semicolon alone.

**1. Independent Clause Joins (Comma, Semicolon, Period, or Conjunction) · medium**

> Marine biologist Ayana Brooks spent a decade recording a single pod of orcas off the coast of Washington ______ her recordings revealed that each family group uses its own distinct set of calls.
>
> Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

- **A. Washington; her** ✓
- B. Washington, her — *trap: Choosing a lone comma between two independent clauses (a comma splice) — one of the most common wrong answers on this pattern.*
- C. Washington her — *trap: Running two independent clauses together with no punctuation at all (a fused sentence).*
- D. Washington, and, her — *trap: Pairing a semicolon with 'and,' 'but,' or 'so' ('; and'), or moving the comma after the conjunction ('and,') — the pattern is a comma before the conjunction, or a semicolon alone.*

*Explanation:* Both sides are complete clauses, so they need a semicolon (or a period). A lone comma is a comma splice, nothing at all is a fused sentence, and 'and,' misplaces the comma.
